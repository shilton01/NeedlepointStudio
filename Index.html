Blocking — do this before finishing anything

Needlepoint always distorts while stitching, so block before you cut or sew. Damp block (spray the back with water until evenly damp, pull square on a padded board with rustproof pins, let dry 24–48 hrs) works for most canvases. Wet block (fully submerge, roll in a towel, then pin square) suits stubborn distortion or wool-heavy pieces — check threads are colorfast first. Steam block (pin square, hover a steam iron just above the surface, never rest it on the stitches) is fastest for small ornaments. Always block face-down on a padded surface, square the corners with a ruler, and let it dry completely flat before moving on.

From Canvas Design → Painted Canvas: Workflow

1. Design — create your canvas art (by hand, digitally, or with an AI image tool); aim for flat colors, bold outlines, limited palette (needlepoint-friendly).

2. Save & log — download the image, then click New Project here and upload it with your specs.

3. Size it — use the Sizing Calculator tab; print the image at exact design size (a full-size print is your painting guide).

4. Transfer — tape the print to a light source, center canvas over it (use the extra margin inches), trace with a waterproof pen, then paint with acrylics thinned so the holes stay open.

5. Stitch — follow this project's thread & stitch tables; the printable stitch guide goes in your project bag.

6. Finish — note materials, tools, and your step-by-step method in the Finishing block so it's ready next time you make this project.

How your Studio works

This is a single file that runs in your web browser. It needs no internet, no account, and no special software — keep needlepoint-studio.html anywhere on your computer (e.g., Documents → Needlepoint) and double-click it to open.

Adding a project: click ＋ New Project, upload (or paste, Ctrl+V) your canvas design or purchased canvas image, and fill in the sizing, threads, stitches, and finishing sections. Everything is optional — you can start with just a title and image and add details later.

How saving works: your browser auto-saves changes as you work, in this browser only. That auto-save is NOT permanent — if you clear your browser data, switch browsers or computers, or something goes wrong, an un-downloaded auto-save can be lost.
To make your changes permanent, you must click the "Save my Studio (download)" button — it's the bold rust-colored button pinned in the top right corner of every screen. Do this any time you've made changes you care about. It downloads a full copy of this entire app (the HTML file itself) with all of your current project data baked directly inside it. That downloaded file is your studio, fully self-contained — replace your old copy with it and you can reopen it anytime, on any computer, with everything intact.
Other backup options (Settings → Data & Backup):

Backup data — exports just your project data (not the whole app) as a small, separate JSON file. It's a lightweight backup or a way to move your data somewhere else — it does not include a working copy of the app itself.

Restore — imports a previously exported JSON backup file back into the app. Restoring replaces whatever projects are currently loaded with the ones from the backup file, so use it to recover from a backup or to load your data into a fresh copy of the app.

Export blank CSV template / Export projects as CSV / Import CSV — for bulk-editing your projects in a spreadsheet, or sharing a log of your work with someone else.
Printing a stitch guide: open any project and click Print stitch guide — you get a clean page with the design image, specs, thread shopping list, and stitch plan to keep in your project bag.

Tip for painting guides: print the design image separately at the exact design size (e.g., 8" × 8") — your Sizing Calculator tab tells you the size to use.

Your Studio lives entirely in this file — remember to click Save my Studio (download) after making changes, and replace your old copy with the new download. Changes are NOT permanently saved until you do this.
